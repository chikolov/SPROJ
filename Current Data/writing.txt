	The statistical analysis in this project is based on three datasets obtained from the Panel Study of Income Dynamics (PSID) Child Development Supplement (CDS), specifically the surveys conducted in 2014, 2019, and 2021. These datasets contain variables related to children’s academic performance, household environment, extracurricular engagement, and participation in music-related and arts-related activities. The inclusion of multiple years provides an opportunity not only to analyze the relationship between music education and academic performance in a specific year, but also to analyze any trends and establish whether correlations remain stable over time. 
	The first part of my analysis consisted of data cleaning and reduction of variables. Only variables directly relevant to music engagement, arts participation, and academic performance were kept from the full PSID datasets. These included variables indicating the presence of musical instruments in the home, participation in music lessons, general music participation, use of electronics for music listening, participation in arts tutoring, involvement in community arts groups, and arts participation in religious settings. In addition, demographic variables such as sex and the variables necessary for calculating normalized GPA were also kept in the cleaned dataset. 
	After isolating the relevant variables, datapoints with missing or inapplicable values in the GPA variables were also removed. In the PSID dataset, GPA values are recorded along with the scale on which the GPA is measured. Because different schools use different grading scales (for example, 4.0 scales, 5.0 scales, 4.5 scales, and other systems), it was necessary to standardize GPA values across all datasets for the sake of clarity. This was accomplished by normalizing GPA as a percentage of the highest possible GPA using the formula:
 GPAnormalized = GPAraw / GPAscale ​
	This normalization ensures that all GPA values fall within the same 0–1 range (0 to 100%), allowing for clarity within the comparisons across different school systems and across the three years included in the study.

GPA Distribution

	The first step of the statistical analysis involved looking into the shape of the distribution of normalized GPA values in each of the three datasets. This step is important because understanding the distribution of the dependent variable provides insight into the performance of the sample and helps determine whether the assumptions of later statistical tests are reasonable.

	In the 2021 dataset, the normalized GPA values produced a mean of 0.809, a median of 0.820, and a standard deviation of 0.142. The distribution is clearly left-skewed, meaning that a large proportion of students cluster near the upper end of the GPA scale while a smaller number of students occupy the lower end. This pattern is consistent with typical academic grading distributions, where the maximum possible GPA constrains the upper limit of the distribution while allowing a longer lower tail.
	Histogram

	The 2019 dataset shows a very similar pattern. The mean normalized GPA in 2019 is 0.793, the median remains at 0.820, and the standard deviation is 0.153. The slightly lower mean GPA compared to 2021 suggests minor variation in academic performance across different years. With that siad, the identical median value indicates that the central tendency of student performance remains largely unchanged.

	Histogram 

	The 2014 dataset exhibits the same general distributional structure. The mean normalized GPA in 2014 is 0.781, the median is 0.786, and the standard deviation is 0.149. Compared with the later datasets, the slightly lower mean and median values could suggest an upward shift in GPA over time, though the overall structure of the distribution remains highly consistent across all three years.

	Histogram

	Taken together, the GPA distributions across the three datasets remain very consistent.  Most students fall within the normalized GPA range of approximately 0.75 to 0.90, which corresponds roughly to letter grades between B- and A– on a traditional four-point scale. The stability of the standard deviation across all three years also indicates that the overall variability in academic performance remains relatively constant across cohorts.


	
T-tests


After the analysis of the distribution of GPA values, the next step of the analysis involved conducting a series of independent t-tests. The purpose of these tests was to determine whether students who participated in specific music-related or arts-related activities exhibited statistically significant differences in academic performance compared to students who did not.
	The t-tests were applied to several binary variables, including participation in music lessons, general music participation, the presence of a musical instrument in the home, participation in arts tutoring, involvement in community arts groups, and participation in arts-related activities within religious settings.
	One of the most consistent results across all three datasets was for the variable representing the presence of a musical instrument in the home. In the 2021 dataset, this variable produced a statistically significant result with t = 3.478 and p = 0.001. The 2019 dataset produced a similar result (t = 2.917, p = 0.004), while the 2014 dataset produced an even stronger result (t = 4.095, p < 0.001). The consistency of this finding across multiple cohorts strongly suggests that the presence of a musical instrument in the household is meaningfully associated with differences in GPA.
	
	Plots of instrument at home for all 3 years

	In contrast, the variable representing participation in music lessons within the last year produced mixed results. In the 2021 dataset, the t-test produced t = 1.498 with p = 0.135, indicating no statistically significant difference in GPA between students who took lessons and those who did not. The 2019 dataset produced a similar result (p = 0.121), while the 2014 dataset produced a statistically significant result (t = 2.157, p = 0.032). This inconsistency suggests that the relationship between music lessons and academic performance may vary across class years or may depend on additional factors outside of the binary variable alone.
	
	Plots for music lessons all 3 years

Participation in general music activities produced non-significant results in most datasets. While the 2021 dataset produced a result approaching statistical significance (p = 0.085), neither the 2019 nor the 2014 datasets showed meaningful differences in GPA between participants and non-participants.

	General music participation all 3 years

The overall result of the t-test results suggest that simple binary indicators of musical participation are not consistently strong predictors of academic performance. However, socioeconomic related factors such as access to a musical instrument appear to have a more stable relationship with GPA outcomes.


Ordinal variables

	In addition to the binary variables, the dataset included two ordinal variables that reported the level of engagement with a certain aspect of music. In contrast to the simple yes/no indicators, ordinal variables provide a more nuanced view of how frequently students engage in music-related activities.

	The first ordinal variable measures the frequency with which students used electronic devices to listen to music within the past 30 days. The distribution of this variable is heavily skewed toward the highest frequency category. In the 2021 dataset  224 students reported listening to music every day, while only a small number of students reported rare or no engagement. A nearly identical distribution is observed in the 2019 dataset and the 2014 dataset.

Correlations

	To further deepen the understanding of the relationship between musical engagement and academic performance, point-biserial correlation coefficients were calculated. This method is appropriate when one variable is continuous (GPA) and the other variable is binary (participation vs. non-participation).

	Across all three datasets, the magnitude of the correlations remains relatively small. Most correlation coefficients fall within the range of 0.07 to 0.20, indicating weak but measurable relationships between music-related variables and GPA.

	The strongest and most consistent correlation across all datasets was observed for the presence of a musical instrument in the home. This is consistent with the t-test observations from earlier. In the 2021 dataset, this variable produced a correlation coefficient of r = −0.196 with p = 0.001. Similar results appear in the 2019 and 2014 datasets, suggesting that this variable is one of the most stable predictors of academic performance among the variables examined.

Multivariable regression analysis

	To better understand the combined influence of multiple variables, a multivariate linear regression model was estimated using the combined dataset from 2014, 2019, and 2021. After removing observations with missing values in any of the variables included in the model, the final sample size consisted of 1,164 students.

	The dependent variable in the model was normalized GPA. Independent variables included participation in music lessons, general music participation, the presence of a musical instrument in the home, frequency of instrument use, electronic music listening, participation in arts tutoring, participation in community arts groups, participation in arts-related activities within religious settings, and a control variable representing survey year.

	The regression model produced an R² value of 0.059, indicating that approximately 5.9% of the variance in GPA is explained by the variables included in the model. While this value is relatively small, it is consistent with expectations in social science research, where academic performance is influenced by many complex and interacting factors.

	Among the predictors included in the model, several variables were statistically significant. The presence of a musical instrument in the home remained a significant predictor of GPA even after controlling for other variables. In similar fashion, participation in arts tutoring, community arts groups, and arts participation within religious settings also produced statistically significant coefficients.

	Surprisingly, variables representing direct music participation, such as music lessons, general music participation, and frequency of music listening, did not produce statistically significant results in the regression model. This finding suggests that the relationship between music and academic performance may operate indirectly through broader environmental factors rather than through music participation alone.

Overall interpretation (needs work)

	Taken together, the results of this analysis suggest that music education is not a strong independent predictor of academic performance when measured using simple binary variables. However, certain environmental factors related to music—particularly the presence of a musical instrument in the home—show a consistent relationship with GPA across multiple datasets.

	This finding supports the idea that music education may function as part of a broader network of social, cultural, and educational influences rather than as a single causal factor. Access to musical instruments, participation in arts activities, and exposure to supportive learning environments may all contribute to academic outcomes in ways that are not fully captured by simple measures of music participation.
